Analysis Rules

3. NEGUS STRATEGY ENTRY MODEL

Every trade must follow a structured sequence.

Step 1 – Establish Market Direction

Analyze D1, H4, and H1. Identify whether the market is Bullish, Bearish, Ranging, or Transitioning.

Step 2 – Identify Key Levels

  • Major support and resistance
  • Previous Day High (PDH)
  • Previous Day Low (PDL)
  • Session Highs and Lows
  • Supply and Demand zones
  • Important liquidity areas

Step 3 – Wait for Price to Reach the Planned Area

Do not chase price. Allow the market to reach a meaningful level before considering an entry.

Step 4 – Confirm on M15

  • Liquidity Sweep
  • Break of Structure (BOS)
  • Change of Character (CHoCH)
  • Strong displacement
  • Clear candle rejection or closing confirmation

Step 5 – Execute on M5

  • Fair Value Gap (FVG) retest
  • Order Block (OB) retest
  • Price-action confirmation
  • Clear structural invalidation

Step 6 – Define SL and TP

Before entering, identify the Stop Loss and Take Profit; calculate potential financial loss; evaluate the reward-to-risk ratio; and check spread and transaction costs.

Step 7 – Execute or Wait

If confirmation is missing, do not enter. No confirmation = No trade.

4. MARKET STRUCTURE AND SMC CONCEPTS

4.1 Break of Structure (BOS)

A break of a meaningful previous swing high or low, generally supporting continuation in the current direction.

4.2 Change of Character (CHoCH)

An early indication that market behavior may be changing. CHoCH is not automatic proof of a full trend reversal.

4.3 Liquidity Sweep

Price moves beyond a visible high or low and then rejects or returns through the level. A sweep becomes more meaningful when followed by displacement and structural confirmation.

4.4 Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A three-candle imbalance where price moves strongly enough to leave an inefficient price area between the first and third candles. A revisit may offer a trading opportunity, but not every FVG must be filled.

4.5 Order Block (OB)

A selected opposing candle or candle cluster before a strong displacement that breaks market structure. The zone is a trading hypothesis and must be validated through testing.

4.6 Premium and Discount

  • Premium: Upper half of the range.
  • Discount: Lower half of the range.
  • Equilibrium: Midpoint of the range.

Use the same swing-selection rules consistently.

5. FUNDAMENTAL AND NEWS ANALYSIS

EUR/USD is influenced by economic conditions and monetary-policy expectations in both the United States and the Eurozone.

5.1 USD News

  • Federal Reserve (Fed)
  • FOMC Interest Rate Decisions
  • Consumer Price Index (CPI)
  • Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE)
  • Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)
  • Unemployment Rate
  • GDP
  • ISM Manufacturing and Services PMI

5.2 EUR News

  • European Central Bank (ECB)
  • ECB Interest Rate Decisions
  • Eurozone CPI
  • GDP
  • PMI
  • Employment Data
  • ECB Speeches

5.3 News Evaluation

  1. Actual data
  2. Forecast
  3. Previous reading
  4. Market expectations
  5. Actual price reaction

A positive headline does not automatically mean the currency will rise. Market expectations and positioning also matter.

5.4 News Protection

  • Avoid impulsive entries immediately before major releases.
  • Watch spread expansion and volatility.
  • Wait for price action to stabilize.
  • Verify the publication time from a reliable economic calendar.

Time convention: Ethiopia uses UTC+3. Session and news schedules should be recorded in Ethiopian local time and UTC. Ethiopian traditional clock notation should be labelled separately from international 12/24-hour time. MT5/Exness server time may differ from UTC; verify the platform clock before converting.

6. RISK MANAGEMENT

Risk management is the foundation of long-term trading survival.

6.1 Mandatory Rules

  1. Every trade must have a defined Stop Loss.
  2. Never widen SL simply to avoid realizing a loss.
  3. Never average down to rescue a losing trade.
  4. Never revenge trade.
  5. Never increase lot size emotionally after a loss.
  6. Avoid opening opposite positions merely to hide a loss.
  7. Account for spread, commission, swap, and slippage.

6.2 Risk Calculation

Approximate formula: Cash Risk = Stop Distance (Pips) × Pip Value × Lot Size

Lot SizeApproximate Value per Pip
0.01$0.10
0.02$0.20
0.03$0.30
0.04$0.40
0.10$1.00

Actual values depend on contract specifications and account currency.

6.3 Example

At 0.01 lot: 10-pip SL ≈ $1; 20-pip SL ≈ $2. With a $15 account: $1 risk ≈ 6.67% of balance; $2 risk ≈ 13.33% of balance.

This demonstrates why small accounts face significant risk when the broker's minimum lot is 0.01.

Important: Never make a structural Stop Loss artificially tight just to fit the account. If the risk is too high, skip the trade or continue practising on demo.

Professional trading requires emotional discipline.

NEGUS Psychological Rules

  • No revenge trading.
  • No chasing missed entries.
  • No trading out of boredom.
  • No unprotected positions.
  • No impulsive lot-size increases.
  • No emotional decisions after losses.
  • No pressure to achieve weekly targets.

Before Every Trade, Ask:

  1. Is the market direction clear?
  2. Is price at a planned area?
  3. Is confirmation present?
  4. Is the Stop Loss logical?
  5. Is the potential loss acceptable?
  6. Is there major news risk?
  7. Am I following my system?

If the answers are unclear, wait.
